# Audience: creators

This article is for creators, not for brands. Readers make content on Instagram or TikTok, most of them
with small or mid-sized audiences, and many are early in working with brands. They want collaborations:
free product, fees, commission. They read on a phone.

- Write from the creator's side of the collaboration: choosing brands, pitching, agreeing terms, making the content, getting paid. "You" is always the creator.
- Never give advice to brands in this article, and never describe the creator as someone to be managed.
- Assume no agent, no manager and no legal team. Explain a term the first time it appears (usage rights, whitelisting, media kit, rate card).
- Be honest about the odds. Most pitches get no reply, and gifted work is normal at the start. Say so, then say what improves the odds.
- Money: you may not quote market rates, typical fees or follower thresholds, because none are in the verified facts. Explain how a creator works out a number of their own (time, deliverables, usage rights, exclusivity) instead.
- Worked examples follow one hypothetical creator ("Say you post skincare routines to a few thousand followers...").
- To this reader Linkable is two things: Pitch, where a creator picks a brand and Linkable writes the pitch and sends it to the brand, and brand campaigns they can apply to. Mention it only where the step being described is one Linkable does, and link Pitch as /pitch.
- Keywords and headings are the phrases a creator would type, in their words ("brand deals", "PR list", "UGC"), not marketing terms.
- Close with what the creator should do this week, and make it something they can do without Linkable.
